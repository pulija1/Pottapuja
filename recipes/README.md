## How the app uses this book

- **Cook by vegetable:** tap one or more vegetables (by their Telugu names) on the Recipes tab and see the family fries and curries made with them, the ones with all the picked vegetables first. ⚡ keeps it to recipes ready in 20 minutes; [telugu-curries.md](telugu-curries.md) has a quick fry and a quick curry for each Telugu vegetable.
- **Ready to cook:** the Recipes tab lists recipes whose ingredients are all in the kitchen list, and the ones that need just one more thing. Water, salt, oil, turmeric and anything used by the pinch don't count.
- **Busy mornings:** on school days the breakfast suggestions are ones made the night before (easy, 20 minutes or less), batch-made at the weekend (only on the days the batch keeps), or with no cooking; nothing is baked or cooked for long that morning, warm ones (with a **Reheat** line) first, each with its side dip (the **Busy morning** line in a recipe), and the baby's breakfast comes with a fruit from the kitchen (a ripe banana when there is none). The Recipes tab's "🌙 Busy morning" tick does the same for "Find recipes", and is on by default on weekdays.
- **Recipes by age:** the Recipes tab has a section each for the 8-month-old, the 4-year-old and the family, with this book's recipes by meal. Tap ☆ on the ones you make all the time: the meal planner picks them first for that person's meal and can bring them back after 2 days instead of waiting a week.
- **Suggested meals:** empty days on the calendar get suggestions picked from this book, best match to the kitchen first, and checked for each child's safety. The cuisines picked in 👤 Profile (Telugu, Tamil, North Indian, Mexican) come first; "South Indian" counts as Telugu and Tamil.
- **Top picks:** the Recipes tab shows the five easiest recipes that use the vegetables in the kitchen, from the picked cuisines.
- **Leftovers:** when the day before had a curry for the family dinner, one suggestion is a makeover from [leftover-makeovers.md](leftover-makeovers.md), like a roti chicken curry quesadilla for the lunchbox.
- **Shopping:** a planned recipe's missing ingredients go to the shopping list with their quantities.
- **Air fryer and oven:** in 👤 Profile → Cooking appliances, tick what the kitchen has. Recipes with a method for it show that method and its time, quick recipes count the faster time, and suggestions lean toward them. With a two-basket air fryer, two air fryer dishes for the same meal time are shown as basket 1 and basket 2 to cook together.
- **AI recipes** (when the server has an API key) are extra. The book works without the AI, including on GitHub Pages where there is no server.

## Nutrition

Each recipe in the app shows calories, protein, carbs, fiber, fat, iron and calcium for one serving, and for the baby's and the 4-year-old's recipes, how much of that child's day one serving covers. The numbers are worked out from the ingredient list (amounts and food values from USDA FoodData Central and the Indian Food Composition Tables, rounded), so they are estimates, not lab values. A recipe added here gets its numbers without any extra work; add a **Serves** line when it makes more than one portion. Rice, pasta and dal are counted dry unless the ingredient says `cooked`; beans are counted cooked unless it says `soaked` or `dry`. New foods the app doesn't know yet go in `public/shared/nutrition.js`.

## Adding or changing a recipe

Copy an existing recipe and change it. The format:

```markdown
### Egg and cheese quesadilla

- **For:** kid
- **Meal:** breakfast
- **Cuisine:** Mexican American
- **Time:** 10 min
- **Good for:** 🥚 Egg · 🥛 Milk/dairy

**Ingredients**

- 1 flour tortilla
- 1 egg, scrambled
- 2 tbsp cheddar cheese, grated
- Salt, to taste

**Steps**

1. Scramble the egg until fully cooked.
2. Fill half the tortilla with egg and cheese, fold, and toast until golden.

**Notes:** Anything you want to remember.
```

- `###` starts a recipe; the title is the recipe's name. `##` headings only group recipes and can be anything.
- **For** is `baby`, `kid` or `family`. **Meal** is `breakfast`, `lunchbox`, `lunch`, `snack`, `dinner` or `side` (a dip or chutney; it is only shown with the dishes that name it, never as a meal on its own). Both are required.
- Optional lines: **Dish** (`fry` or `curry`: the **Cook by vegetable** card lists the recipe under that heading; without it the title decides, so "vepudu" or "fry" is a fry and "kura", "pulusu" or "pappu" a curry), **Cuisine** (two can be named for fusion, like `Telugu / Mexican fusion`), **Leftover** (what yesterday's dish it uses: `chicken curry`, `paneer curry`, `beans curry`, `vegetable curry`, `dal`, `sambar` or `any curry`), **Time**, **Serves** (how many portions it makes, for the nutrition numbers; `Serves: 6 (1 muffin each)`. Without it, a baby or kid recipe is one portion and a family recipe is four), **Texture** (baby: `puree`, `mashed`, `porridge`, `soft finger food`), **Busy morning** (`make ahead` for a breakfast made the night before, `no cook` for one with no cooking: weekday breakfast suggestions use these first), **Prep ahead** (for a make-ahead breakfast, when and how long the work is, and how long it keeps: `night before · 15 min` or `weekend · 35 min · bake a tray, keeps 4 days in the fridge`. Night-before work over 20 minutes counts as weekend prep, and a weekend batch is only suggested on the weekdays it keeps for), **Reheat** (how to warm a make-ahead breakfast in the morning, `1 min in the microwave`; warm ones come first on weekdays), **Side dip** (the titles of dips from the book to serve with it, comma separated), **Nut-free swap** (what to do if the school goes nut-free), **Air fryer** and **Oven** (how to make it in that appliance, starting with the total time and the temperature: `- **Air fryer:** 15 min at 200°C. Toss the okra in 2 tsp oil, shake every 5 minutes.`), **Family favorite:** yes, and **ID** (only for older recipes, so plans that already use them keep working).
- **Good for** is for people reading the file. The app works out egg, milk/dairy and nuts from the ingredients by itself.
- Each ingredient is one line: amount, unit, name, then a comma and any note. `1½ cups rice, washed`, `2 tbsp almond powder`, `6 curry leaves`, `Salt, to taste`. Units the app understands: g, kg, ml, l, tsp, tbsp, cup, pinch, slice, clove, sprig, handful, inch, bunch, can, packet, dozen. Keep the name the way you'd write it on a shopping list (`paneer`, `cheddar cheese`, `black beans`) so it matches the kitchen list.
- Steps are a numbered list.
- If a recipe can't be read (no **For**, no ingredients or no steps), the app skips it and says which file and line on the Recipes tab.

### Safety rules the app checks

Every recipe is checked when the app loads it, and the tests in `tests/recipe-book.test.js` fail if a recipe in this book breaks a rule:

- **Baby:** no honey, no added salt or sugar (or jaggery), no whole or chopped nuts (fine powder or a thin nut butter only), eggs fully cooked, nothing hard or round (whole grapes, raw carrot sticks, whole peas). Write "no salt" or "unsalted" where it matters; the checker understands that.
- **4-year-old:** nuts chopped, slivered, powdered or as a butter, never whole. Grapes and cherry tomatoes cut lengthwise. No caffeine.
- **School nut-free** (a setting in the app, off by default): lunchbox and snack recipes with nuts are hidden or swapped.

General guidance only, not medical advice. Check new foods and allergies with your paediatrician.
